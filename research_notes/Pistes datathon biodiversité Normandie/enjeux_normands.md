## 1. Bocage & grasslands: hedgerow loss since the 1970s, recent trends, links with livestock decline and grassland ploughing

### Takeaway
Normandy is still France's third-densest hedgerow region, with about 158,000 km of hedges and about twice the national density. It has nevertheless lost about half its hedgerows since 1972. Between 2008 and 2019 it lost about 11% of its grasslands (103,000 ha), mostly ploughed for crops as dairy systems change. Its permanent-grassland ratio fell faster than in any other region (−6.45% between the 2018 reference and 2023), above the threshold for the strictest CAP regime.

### Cited Findings
Hedgerows
- Normandy has 158,433 km of hedges, a density of 53 m/ha against 27 m/ha in metropolitan France. It is the 3rd densest region after Bretagne and Pays de la Loire, and the Manche is France's densest department at 96 m/ha. The data come from the Dispositif de suivi des bocages (2004–2022 imagery), in a document published in 2024 ‡ — [DREAL/ANBDD, Linéaires de haies Normandie 2024](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/lineaireshaies-normandie-2024_anbdd.pdf)
- A 2025 colloquium presentation gives about 158,000 km in Normandy, −50% between 1972 and 2016, or about −1.5% per year. It quotes a density of 57 m/ha against 28 m/ha nationally, a slightly different reference from the DREAL/ANBDD document ‡ — [Colloquium presentation 2025, hosted on Canal-U](https://www.canal-u.tv/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2025/05/PRESENTATION%20COLLOQUE.pdf)
- In the Manche, density fell from about 175 m/ha in 1972 to 87 m/ha in 2015, half the linear length lost in 45 years. The report (Delahaye, Université de Caen/CNRS, and Debray, ANBDD, dated 22 Jan 2025) reviews 1970–2023 ‡ — [GIEC normand report no. 2, "Haies et bocages"](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/giec2_haies_bocage_vf_22janv2025.pdf)
- The DREAL statistical bocage study photo-interprets hedges inside 300 m-radius sample circles. It produces a linear index (km/km²) and a mesh-coherence index, with reports for 1972, 1984, 1997-98, 2006, 2010 and 2012. GIS layers with hedge appearance and disappearance years are open data — [data.gouv.fr, Analyse statistique de la dynamique bocagère](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/analyse-statistique-de-la-dynamique-bocagere-cercles-statistiques-normandie)
- The Manche has about 55,000 km of hedges and plants 40–50 km a year through the Chambre d'agriculture and planting associations. In winter 2020-21, 42 km of flat hedges and 12 km of hedges on banks (talus) were supported (article of 4 Feb 2021) — [Pleinchamp](https://www.pleinchamp.com/actualite/40-a-50-km-de-haies-plantees-par-an-dans-la-manche)
- National comparison:
  - France lost an average of 23,571 km of hedges a year in 2017–2021, against 10,400 km a year in 2006–2014, while plantings add about 3,000 km a year (CGAAER report, April 2023) ‡ — [Pleinchamp](https://www.pleinchamp.com/actualite/la-france-perd-encore-plus-de-20-000-km-de-haies-par-an)
  - 70% of the hedges of French bocages have gone since 1950 ‡ — [Réussir](https://www.reussir.fr/haies-un-rapport-ministeriel-confirme-leur-diminution-constante)
  - IGN puts France's total at about 1.55 million km ‡ — [notre-planete.info](https://www.notre-planete.info/actualites/17-benefices-planter-haies-bocage)
  - The Pacte en faveur de la haie targets a net gain of 50,000 km by 2030 ‡ — [Assemblée nationale amendment](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/16/amendements/2600/AN/3657.pdf)
- Hedge junctions hold on average 3.7 hedge-edge bird species, against 2.2 along straight stretches (citing the GIEC normand report) ‡ — [Caen la Mer](https://caenlamer.fr/bonne-haie-bon-endroit)

Grasslands and livestock
- Between 2008 and 2019, Normandy lost about 11% of its agricultural grasslands (permanent and temporary), or 103,000 ha — [Agreste Études no. 6, DRAAF Normandie, Sept 2021](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Agreste_Etudes_no6-Prairies_Normandie_2008_2019-septembre_2021_cle09a9b1.pdf). The same study finds:
  - Grasslands still covered 809,000 ha in 2019, out of about 2 million ha of agricultural land.
  - Calvados, Manche and Orne lost 71,000 ha, 69% of the regional loss, and gained 62,000 ha of arable land.
  - The loss is concentrated in specialised and mixed dairy systems. In the west, grassland was ploughed for cereals and oilseeds; in the east, for industrial crops (beet, flax) and potatoes.
- The same study's OSCOM 2008–2019 table gives, for Normandy: "prairies and other agricultural soils" −107,799 ha, arable land +88,641 ha and artificialised land +22,421 ha. Of the grassland lost, 82% went to arable land and 18% was artificialised, in periurban areas, on the coast, along the Seine axis and for roads such as the A88 and A150 — [Agreste Études no. 6](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Agreste_Etudes_no6-Prairies_Normandie_2008_2019-septembre_2021_cle09a9b1.pdf)
- Between 2000 and 2020, the Manche had the strongest rise in cereal and oilseed (COP) area of any Normandy department (+38%). COP still covered only 15% of its farmland in 2020, against 39% regionally — same source
- The ratio of permanent grassland to farmland (SAU), set under CAP rule BCAE 1, fell 6.45% in Normandy between the 2018 reference and 2023. This is the steepest drop of the four regions above 2% (Grand-Est −3.42%, Bretagne −4.60%, Pays de la Loire −5.20%). The article's rule is that a drop above 5% triggers the conversion-ban regime (arrêté of 31 Oct 2023) — [Réussir Lait, 26 Oct 2023, updated 6 Nov 2023](https://www.reussir.fr/lait/prairies-permanentes-4-regions-en-regime-dautorisation-ou-dinterdiction)
- Normandy was already put under prior authorisation for grassland conversion in 2017, when the ratio was −3.55% against 2012 [older] — [Terre-net, 10 Nov 2017](https://www.terre-net.fr/reglementation/article/132187/la-normandie-et-les-hauts-de-france-epingles-pour-retournement-excessif)
- Land cover (Teruti-Lucas; data year not stated): permanent grassland (STH) covers 21% of Normandy against 14% of provincial France; agricultural land covers 69%, the highest of any region; wooded land 17% against 31% — [INSEE Analyses Normandie no. 80, 9 Jul 2020](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/4629011)
- Livestock driver: the Region's "Plan Reconquête Élevage 2024–2027" (council meeting of 25 Mar 2024) cites a record loss of 5,000 suckler cows between 2021 and 2022. It links cattle-herd decapitalisation to the loss of grasslands, landscapes and associated biodiversity, and cites −12% for the national cattle herd in 6 years — [Région Normandie draft deliberation AP 24-AGR-07-03-10](https://www.politique-animaux.fr/sites/www.politique-animaux.fr/fichiers/projet_delib-reconquete_elevage-normandie.pdf)
- Normandy had more than 26,500 farms in 2020 (first results of the 2020 agricultural census) ‡ — [Agreste Études no. 9, Dec 2021](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/20211210_Agreste_Etudes_no9-Premiers_Resultats_RA_2020_Normandie.pdf)
- The regional scientific council (CSRPN) adopted a motion on grasslands on 12 Dec 2024; its content was not read — [CSRPN motion](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/motion_csrpn_2024-12-12_prairies_vfsigne.pdf)

### Inferences
- −1.5% a year is Normandy's 1972–2016 average (0.5^(1/44) ≈ −1.56% a year) and also the national 2017–2021 rate. On 158,000 km, that rate would mean about 2,400 km lost a year, roughly 7 times the 2024 Pacte haie planting target of 320 km (see §9). This is a computed order of magnitude, not a measured recent net balance for Normandy.
- Normandy holds about 10% of France's hedges (158,000 of 1.55 million km), which fits its density of about twice the national average.
- Grassland ploughing is the shared driver behind hedge removal when fields are enlarged, erosion and runoff (§4), nitrate and pesticide transfer (§3), the decline of farmland birds (§7) and land take (§5, where grasslands are the main land artificialised). Pistes C, B, L, N and E can all rest on this one causal chain.
- −50% hedges since 1972, −11% grasslands in a decade and the worst grassland-ratio drop in France make "bocage and grasslands" the most Normandy-specific and best-documented issue.

### Gaps
- The exact DREAL index values for 1972, 1984, 1998, 2006, 2010 and 2012 (by department) were not retrieved because the PDFs are blocked. The 1972→2006 linear-index change therefore cannot be quoted.
- No measured net hedge balance for Normandy after 2016 (plantings minus removals) was found.
- No change in permanent-grassland area between the 2010 and 2020 censuses was retrieved for Normandy.
- The departmental grassland and herd figures in the Region's livestock deliberation are unreadable (garbled fonts) and were not used.

## 2. Wetlands: extent and losses (Marais du Cotentin et du Bessin, Seine estuary and valleys), peatlands, drained areas

### Takeaway
Normandy has nationally important wetlands: the 30,000 ha Marais du Cotentin et du Bessin (Ramsar site of about 38,900 ha, with peatlands) and the Seine estuary and valley. Published regional loss statistics are scarce. The best-quantified loss is the Seine estuary, whose water surface shrank from 340 km² in 1750 to 142 km² in 2005.

### Cited Findings
- The Parc naturel régional des Marais du Cotentin et du Bessin covers 146,650 ha, including a 30,000 ha wetland, the largest in Normandy ‡ — [DREAL regional park sheet FR8000021](https://www.donnees.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/pdf/PNR/FR8000021f.pdf)
- Ramsar site no. 516 (Marais du Cotentin et du Bessin, Baie des Veys) has an official area of 38,891 ha (39,938 ha on the summary sheet). It contains about 2,000 ha of peaty grasslands and peat bogs, and 7,000–8,000 ha of outcropping peat ‡ — [Ramsar Information Sheet FR516](https://rsisd7.unepgrid.ch/RISapp/files/RISrep/FR516RIS_2307_fr.pdf)
- A CGEDD (ministry inspectorate) report estimates the emblematic wet area of these marshes at 34,591 ha. The perimeters differ between sources ‡ — [CGEDD report, Marais du Cotentin et du Bessin](https://cgedd.documentation.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/documents/Affaires-0013429/014422-01P_rapport-publie-Marais%20du%20Contentin%20et%20du%20Bessin.pdf)
- The Seine estuary's water surface went from 340 km² in 1750 to 142 km² in 2005. The Poses–Oissel and Rouen–Le Havre sectors have been halved since 1750 [older] ‡ — [GIP Seine-Aval, DTA environmental indicators, 2017 update](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/gipsa_indicateurs_dta_2017.pdf)
- The estuary's intertidal mudflats declined sharply during the 20th century, and a little over 1,000 ha remain ‡ — [DREAL Normandie synthesis](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/synthese_objectif_2.pdf)
- Impoverished mudflats reduce the estuary's nursery capacity for sea bass and sole, though it remains France's leading sea-bass nursery (2019) ‡ — [COPIL REPERE minutes, 5 Jun 2019](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/cr_copil_repere_du_2019-06-05_-_.pdf)
- In the Seine valley, alluvial wetlands and peaty marshes "have strongly declined for several decades". For example, the Marais de la Harelle at Heurteauville (over 300 ha) is now partly wooded ‡ — [INPN ZNIEFF 230031044](https://inpn.mnhn.fr/docs/ZNIEFF/znieffpdf/230031044.pdf)
- Infilling, drying, drainage and polderisation are listed as pressures in regional ZNIEFF sheets, for example the Marais de la Mare du Daim (about 20 ha) ‡ — [INPN ZNIEFF 250015920](https://inpn.mnhn.fr/zone/znieff/250015920?lg=en)
- The regional wetland inventory is coordinated by the DREAL and combines photo-interpretation with field inventories. It comes with a model of land predisposed to wetlands (MPPZH: strongly or weakly predisposed). The dataset was updated on 15 Nov 2024 ‡ — [data.gouv.fr, Zones humides Normandie](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/zones-humides-normandie)
- Transitional (estuarine) waters were only 14.3% in good ecological status in 2015, and 28.6% were in bad status [older] — [INSEE 2020](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/4629011)

### Inferences
- The estuary has lost about 58% of its water surface since 1750 ((340 − 142) / 340). This works as a single "human footprint" headline for pistes M and G.
- Normandy's wetlands are concentrated in the Cotentin–Bessin marshes, the Seine valley and estuary, the Pays de Bray peat bogs and the coastal marshes. Piste G could cross the MPPZH predisposition layer with current land use (arable land, artificialised land), but the pitch would have no regional "x% lost" headline.

### Gaps
- No published regional figure was found for total wetland area as a share of the territory, historical loss rate, total peatland area or drained farmland area. The search budget ran out before the 2020 census drainage figure could be looked up.
- No quantified outcome of wetland-restoration programmes in Normandy (CEN Normandie, regional parks, water agency) was found.

## 3. Rivers & water: ecological status, obstacles, migratory fish, Sélune dam removal, catchments, green tides and shellfish

### Takeaway
Water is Normandy's most acute and best-quantified pollution issue. Groundwater is chemically degraded at 60% of monitoring points, against 31% nationally. Across the Seine-Normandie basin, only 18–23% of rivers would be in good ecological status in the provisional 2025 assessment. Ninety-eight drinking-water catchments are "priority" for diffuse agricultural pollution. Migratory salmon are collapsing (−72%), while the Sélune dam removals are a flagship recovery.

### Cited Findings
Ecological and chemical status
- 2015 data from INSEE (published 2020) [older] — [INSEE Analyses Normandie no. 80](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/4629011):
  - 43% of Normandy's surface-water monitoring points were in good or very good ecological status, against 44.3% in metropolitan France.
  - By water type: rivers 43.7%, coastal waters 63%, transitional waters 14.3%, lakes 11.1%.
  - Groundwater was in good quantitative status at 97% of points, but not in good chemical status at 60% of points, against 31% nationally.
- 42% of Normandy's surface waters were in good ecological status, against 30% for the whole Seine-Normandie basin. The basin fell from 32% in 2019 to 30% in the 2022 intermediate assessment ‡ — [CESER Normandie, water summary sheet, 2023](https://ceser.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/Fiche%20de%20synth%C3%A8se%20-%20EAU_0.pdf)
- Provisional 2025 assessment (état des lieux) for the Seine-Normandie basin ‡ — [CESER Normandie contribution, Mar 2025](https://www.ceser.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2025-03/Consultation%20eau%20CESER%20Web_2.pdf):
  - Only 18–23% of rivers would be in good ecological status, against 30% in 2022 and 32% in 2019.
  - Only 20–25% of groundwater would be in good chemical status.
  - Part of the fall comes from method changes, including new indicators such as pesticide metabolites.
  - The Water Framework Directive (DCE) good-status goal will not be met in 2027.
- The 2022–2027 basin plan (SDAGE) targets 53% of water bodies in good ecological status by 2027 ‡ — [DRIEAT presentation](https://www.drieat.ile-de-france.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/presentation_edl_idf_vf2.pdf)

Drinking-water catchments
- Catchment status in 2020 (the mapping of each figure to its document should be re-checked) ‡ — [DREAL Normandie, catchments note, June 2020](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/20200623_captage-ep_vf.pdf):
  - 98 catchments are "priority" for action against diffuse pollution.
  - 317 of 1,159 catchments are "sensitive" to diffuse pollution by nitrates and pesticides.
  - 43% of priority catchments are in poor state for nitrates and/or pesticides.
  - 60% of priority catchments are at risk of not reaching good status by 2027.
- Abandoned catchments ‡ — [ARS Normandie, 2019 report](https://www.normandie.ars.sante.fr/system/files/2020-12/20115681-BAT%20ARS%2032%20pages%20HD-O.pdf); [ARS Normandie, 2021 report](https://www.grainville-odon.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Bilan-Eau-2021.pdf):
  - 32 abandoned (2.8% of 1,158) in the 2019 figures.
  - 37 abandoned or with protection procedures dropped (about 3% of 1,152) as of Jan 2022, the Orne being most affected (24 of 172).
  - Rising pesticide non-compliance is linked mainly to the metabolite ESA-metolachlor in western Normandy's resources.
- By end 2022, 91% of priority-catchment feeding areas were delineated and 68% had an action programme, against 83% and 53% in 2019 ‡ — [ARS Normandie](https://www.normandie.ars.sante.fr/media/115043/download)
- At end 2022 there were 1,147 active catchments, 1,113 of them groundwater, and 92% are protected by a declaration of public utility (DUP). Nitrate exceedances in distributed water are rare, but concentrations are rising in parts of the region (2024 report, published Dec 2025) ‡ — [ARS Normandie](https://www.normandie.ars.sante.fr/media/147002/download)
- In 2011, about 339,000 people in Seine-Maritime (about 30% of its population) received water above the pesticide limit. The source is an advocacy note [older] ‡ — [EELV Haute-Normandie note, Jan 2013](https://hautenormandie.eelv.fr/files/2013/01/Note08012013_pollutionEauHN.pdf)

River continuity and migratory fish
- The former regional ecological coherence schemes (SRCE) identified 1,735 obstacles to flow in Haute-Normandie and 1,600 in Basse-Normandie. These inventories date from the 2009 basin plans [older data, cited in 2024] ‡ — [SRADDET Normandie annexes 4, 29 Mar 2024](https://www.prefectures-regions.gouv.fr/normandie/irecontenu/telechargement/117234/874029/file/2024-03-29%20(DT)%20-%20SRADDET%20annexes%204.pdf)
- Atlantic salmon in the Seine-Normandie basin: abundances fell 72% in 2023–2024 compared with 2010–2019, and 2025 had the lowest spawner returns since 2008. Salmon fishing remains banned across the basin. These figures come from search excerpts of DRIEAT/COGEPOMI regulatory documents ‡ — [DRIEAT consultation note 2026-27](https://www.drieat.ile-de-france.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/note_presentation_consultation_public_26-27.pdf); [order on the salmon fishing ban](https://www.drieat.ile-de-france.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/m2024d2922_ap_sn_interdiction_saumon_2025_vf.pdf)
- In 2022, rod catches were the lowest in ten years: 51 salmon (Haute-Normandie and Seine-Normandie), about 6 of them on the Touques ‡ — [COGEPOMI opinion, 28 Nov 2023](https://www.drieat.ile-de-france.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/231128_avis_cogepomi_projet_ap_cadre_peche_bassin_2024-2025.pdf)

Sélune dam removal
- The two hydro dams, La Roche-qui-Boit (16 m, 1920) and Vezins (36 m, 1932), were no longer profitable and blocked migratory fish. The scientific programme began in 2012 (AFB–INRAE Rennes), and migratory fish had been studied on the Oir tributary since 1983. The dams held back sediment and phosphorus but not nitrates (2018) — [Sciences Ouest no. 358, 15 Jan 2018](https://espace-sciences.org/sciences-ouest/358/actualite/un-fleuve-reprend-son-cours)
- Vezins was razed in 2019 and La Roche-qui-Boit in 2022. Sources give "2019–2023" for the whole project. The removal reconnects about 60 km of the main river, 20 km of which had been drowned by the reservoirs ‡ — [Ifremer (Archimer)](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00943/105443/118113.pdf); [INRAE (HAL)](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-04839973v1/document)
- Before removal, an estimated 200–700 adult salmon a year were confined below the dams. In 2022, 2 of 18 radio-tagged salmon went above the former dams, and one stayed more than 20 km upstream of La Roche-qui-Boit for the whole spawning season. In 2023, scientific electrofishing found young salmon born upstream, though few of them after a poor 2022. The source is popular press quoting INRAE ‡ — [Demotivateur](https://www.demotivateur.fr/environnement/la-france-rase-deux-barrages-de-16-et-36-metres-pres-du-mont-saint-michel-des-le-printemps-suivant-des-saumons-naissent-la-ou-l-espece-avait-disparu-depuis-un-siecle-50637)
- Observed outcomes, from Sciences Ouest no. 414 (30 Nov 2023) — [Sciences Ouest no. 414](https://espace-sciences.org/sciences-ouest/414/actualite/le-retour-des-poissons-dans-la-selune):
  - The last dam was removed at the end of 2022.
  - European eels and lamprey spawning sites were found in several sectors upstream of the former dams, along with young Atlantic salmon, which the article calls proof of recolonisation and reproduction.
  - Water downstream has cooled by 2 °C and holds more oxygen.
  - Pollution-sensitive mayflies and stoneflies have returned.
  - About 60 scientists are involved, and monitoring continues until at least 2027.
- Allis shad may have returned to the Sélune, but this is unconfirmed ‡ — [Reporterre](https://reporterre.net/Un-poisson-fantome-a-l-affut-de-la-grande-alose-celle-qu-on-ne-voit-pas)

Coastal waters, shellfish and algae
- Normandy is France's largest shellfish-farming basin, and the Region has funded aid for oyster farmers hit by repeated norovirus crises ‡ — [Région Normandie press release](https://rn136.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2024-03/communique-norovirus2024-soutien-normandie.pdf)
- On 29 Dec 2023, two prefectoral orders temporarily banned the sale of shellfish from Grandcamp-Maisy, Géfosse-Fontenay and Saint-Vaast-la-Hougue. The Baie des Veys oyster zones had to close in both 2023 and 2024 because of norovirus ‡ — [Région Normandie press release, Sept 2024](https://rn136.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2024-09/communique-rencontre-ostreiculteurs-calvados.pdf)
- In winter 2019–2020, 23 shellfish zones were closed in 7 departments, including 2 in Calvados and 2 in Manche. The closures were linked to heavy rains combined with the winter gastroenteritis epidemic, which points to sewage [older] ‡ — [Ministère de l'Agriculture](https://agriculture.gouv.fr/telecharger/106685)
- Normandy's algal strandings are mostly the "arrachage" type: seaweed torn from rocky platforms by storms and swell. Brittany's green tides, by contrast, grow in place [older] ‡ — [CGDD, Le Point sur no. 180](https://temis.documentation.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/docs/Temis/0079/Temis-0079790/Point_180.pdf)

### Inferences
- The groundwater chemical-status contrast (60% of Normandy points not good against 31% nationally) is the strongest Normandy-versus-France contrast found in this research. It is a prime pitch figure for piste E.
- The salmon decline affects the whole basin, including rivers without large dams. This suggests drivers beyond obstacles, such as marine survival or temperature, which were not covered by the sources found. Continuity work (piste F) is necessary but not sufficient. The Sélune is best used as proof of a positive practice (P2) rather than as the most acute pressure.
- Shellfish closures in Normandy are mainly microbial (norovirus reaching the sea through sewage after rain). They link more to wastewater and urban pressure than to agriculture, which bears on the scope of pistes E and M.

### Gaps
- The final adopted figures of the 2025 assessment were not found, nor a breakdown for Normandy alone.
- No current count or density of obstacles from the national obstacle register (ROE) was found for Normandy, nor a regional tally of obstacles removed or equipped.
- No trend for eel or shad abundance in Normandy was found.
- No Sélune counts for 2024–2025 were read. An "Observatoire des biocénoses aquatiques de la Sélune" 2025 activity report is listed in the GéoSAS catalogue but was not opened.
- No recent quantified data on green tides or algal strandings (2020–2025) in Normandy was found.

## 4. Soils: erosion and muddy floods (ruissellement, coulées boueuses) in the Pays de Caux and Eure

### Takeaway
The crusting silty soils of the Pays de Caux are among France's most erosion-prone. In high-risk zones, 1–10 t/ha of soil move each year (5–10 t/ha under potatoes), and 0.3–0.7 t/ha a year leaves fields for rivers and villages as mudflows and drinking-water turbidity. This links directly to the loss of grassland and hedges and to crop choice, but recent regional quantification is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Figures from the director of AREAS, the regional runoff and erosion association (Saint-Valery-en-Caux), in an article of 3 Dec 2018 [older] — [Réussir Grandes Cultures](https://www.reussir.fr/grandes-cultures/levee-de-microbarrages-entre-les-buttes):
  - In high-risk zones (Pays de Caux, Artois), 1–10 t/ha of soil move at least 1 m each year on average.
  - Under potatoes the figure is 5–10 t/ha a year.
  - 0.3–0.7 t/ha a year leaves the field for rivers or villages, mainly clay, organic matter, fertilisers and pesticides.
  - A Caux farmer lost about 100 t/ha (1,000 t on a 10 ha field) in 2012–13.
  - Micro-dams built between potato ridges can hold storms of up to 40 mm.
- In the Pays de Caux, crusting silts ("limons battants") cause runoff and concentrated erosion in thalwegs. The results are mudflows, which also hit communes without rivers, and turbidity in drinking water. Three-quarters of the territory is affected by water erosion ‡ — [AFES, Étude et Gestion des Sols 14-3](https://afes.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/EGS_14_3_EGS_14_3_Lheriteau.pdf)
- After the floods and mudflows of June 2019, communes in Calvados, the Orne and Seine-Maritime were recognised as natural disasters (CatNat order of Oct 2019) ‡ — [Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/crues-et-inondations-etat-de-catastrophe-naturelle-reconnu-pour-une-trentaine-de-communes)

### Inferences
- In the Caux plateau, erosion connects piste C (hedges and banks as runoff breaks), piste E (turbidity, pesticide transfer to catchments) and piste L (hedge-planting scenarios on thalwegs). It strengthens the bocage story with a "service" argument.

### Gaps
- No recent (2020–2026) regional count of CatNat "floods and mudflows" orders or damage costs was found.
- No AREAS regional erosion map or recent tonnage figures were retrieved, and no figures for the Eure.

## 5. Artificialisation & brownfields: land take, comparison with France, ZAN target, brownfields

### Takeaway
Normandy keeps converting about 1,800–2,000 ha a year of natural and farm land: 17,700 ha in 2011–2021, mainly for housing (71%) and mostly grassland. It uses land less "efficiently" than France as a whole: jobs fell while land was consumed. Artificialisation sped up again in 2018–2023, and 2023 was the second-worst year since 2008. The Region's ZAN halving envelope is about 6,000 ha for 2021–2030, on the regional CCF baseline. EPF Normandie lists about 5,100 brownfields covering about 3,300 ha.

### Cited Findings
- Land take, efficiency and ZAN, from France Stratégie (Oct 2024; land files from Cerema, 2022 vintage) — [France Stratégie, regional ZAN focus on Normandy, Oct 2024](https://www.strategie-plan.gouv.fr/files/2024-10/fs-na_zan-focus_region_normandie_final.pdf):
  - Normandy consumed 17,700 ha of natural, agricultural and forest land in 2011–2021: 71% for housing, 18% for economic activity and 7% for infrastructure.
  - In 2011 alone it consumed 2,412 ha, 1,770 ha of it for housing.
  - Median consumption per intercommunality (EPCI) over 2011–2021 was 193 ha in Normandy against 134 ha in metropolitan France.
  - Median household growth was +5.5% against +7.0% nationally, and median job growth −1.1% against +1.7%.
  - Twelve EPCIs combine high consumption with low efficiency, mainly north of Le Havre and in the Seine valley. For example, the CA de la Région Dieppoise gained 2.2 households and lost 19 private jobs per hectare consumed.
  - Three Perche and L'Aigle intercommunalities lost more than 30 jobs per hectare consumed for economic activity.
  - More than 5,500 ha (31% of consumption) could have been saved had each commune matched the median efficiency of its urbanity group.
  - About 5,700 ha of potential brownfields without a project were identified in the region (Cartofriches inventory), 6% of France's brownfields.
  - The envelope for projects of national scope is about 800 ha, and the communal guarantee up to about 500 ha, together about 7% of the 2021–2031 envelope.
  - Normandy was one of the first two regions to adopt its ZAN amendment to the regional plan (SRADDET), in May 2023.
- On the Region's own baseline (the EPF Normandie land-consumption map, CCF), about 12,000 ha were consumed in 2011–2020, which leaves about 6,000 ha for 2021–2030 ‡ — [Normandie à la carte, SRADDET objective](https://normandie-a-la-carte.normandie.fr/sobriete_fonciere/objectif_sraddet/o4bis.pdf)
- The amended SRADDET was voted on 25 Mar 2024 and approved by the regional prefect on 31 May 2024. Twelve local plans (SCoT) are being amended and 5 have been amended, while most municipal plans (PLU/PLUi) have not started. The Region's 2025 review covers only the CCF 2021 and 2022 vintages ‡ — [Région Normandie, Bilan ENAF 2025 (Apr 2026)](https://www.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2026-04/bilan_enaf_2025.pdf)
- OSCOM land-use series, 2008–2023 — [Agreste Études no. 19, DRAAF Normandie, Mar 2026](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/acces-etudes-19_isag-oscom.pdf):
  - Artificialised land grew by 29,287 ha (+11.8%) to 277,185 ha, a gain of about 2,000 ha a year.
  - Agricultural land fell by 26,984 ha (−1.3%) and natural land by 2,304 ha (−0.34%).
  - Farmland losses sped up again: −8,954 ha in 2008–13, −7,610 ha in 2013–18 and −10,421 ha in 2018–23.
  - 2023 lost 2,786 ha of farmland, the second-largest annual loss after 2011 (−3,985 ha).
  - Farmland change by department, 2008–2023: Calvados −1.9% (−7,462 ha), Manche −1.6% (−7,103 ha), Seine-Maritime −1.6% (−6,689 ha), Eure −0.9% (−3,705 ha), Orne −0.5% (−2,025 ha).
- Net land-use flows 2008–2023, from the same study — [Agreste Études no. 19](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/acces-etudes-19_isag-oscom.pdf):
  - 23,580 ha went from farmland to artificial uses, 0.78% of the region.
  - 5,700 ha went from natural to artificial uses, concentrated in the Seine valley: Métropole Rouen Normandie 396 ha, Seine Normandie Agglomération 247 ha, CA Seine-Eure 239 ha, Le Havre Seine Métropole 243 ha.
  - 22 EPCIs are at least 10% artificialised: Rouen, Le Havre, Évreux, Caen, Cherbourg, Granville, Alençon.
  - 3,400 ha went from farmland to natural land, nearly half of it in the Manche.
- Artificialised land covers 11% of Normandy against 9% of provincial France, the 4th highest share among metropolitan regions (Teruti-Lucas; published 2020) — [INSEE](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/4629011)
- Artificialisation of farmland happens mostly at the expense of grassland: 18% of the grassland lost in 2008–2019 was artificialised — [Agreste Études no. 6, 2021](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Agreste_Etudes_no6-Prairies_Normandie_2008_2019-septembre_2021_cle09a9b1.pdf)
- EPF Normandie brownfield observatory:
  - About 5,093 sites covering 3,322 ha, 3,761 of them without a project, more than 700 contributions in a year, and an update campaign planned for 2026–2027 (early-2026 presentation) ‡ — [EPF Normandie presentation](https://cdn.netboard.me/151942/files/684d8ffb563dd10dd0fe726ccacad56d.pdf)
  - About 5,100 sites and 3,319 ha, with about 270 sites updated since the start of 2026. Each site has a decision-aid sheet scoring possible uses, including renaturation ‡ — [Prefecture of region, COP planning document](https://www.prefectures-regions.gouv.fr/irecontenu/telechargement/141551/1029345/file/Info%20Cop)

### Inferences
- The brief's figure of 5,068 brownfields is an earlier extract of the same observatory, which now lists about 5,093–5,100 sites.
- The brownfield stock (about 3,300 ha, about 0.1% of the region) equals less than two years of artificialisation at about 2,000 ha a year. Renaturing brownfields (piste A) is symbolically strong and relevant to ZAN, but small compared with the yearly flow. France Stratégie's 5,700 ha uses a different inventory (Cartofriches).
- Two official baselines coexist: 17,700 ha in 2011–2021 from land files, and about 12,000 ha in 2011–2020 from the regional CCF. A pitch must say which one it uses.
- Normandy's land take is not explained by demand: household growth below the national median and job losses alongside median EPCI consumption 44% above France's (193 against 134 ha). This is a strong "inefficient land take" narrative for piste I.

### Gaps
- The exact 2021–2024 consumption figures in the Region's Bilan ENAF 2025 could not be read (403).
- No m² per inhabitant comparison with France was found as such.

## 6. Coast & Seine estuary: cliff retreat, dune erosion, sea-level-rise exposure, estuary habitats, port and industry pressure

### Takeaway
Normandy's coast is retreating. Chalk cliffs lose about 20 cm a year on average and over 40 cm a year in places, and west-Cotentin dunes lose metres per stormy season. Two-thirds of the coastline is exposed to sea-level rise, which concerns about 122,000 inhabitants. The Seine estuary concentrates port and industrial pressure on intertidal habitats.

### Cited Findings
- Seine-Maritime chalk cliffs (Côte d'Albâtre) ‡ — [DDTM 76 public brochure](https://www.seine-maritime.gouv.fr/index.php/contenu/telechargement/38432/257237/file/Plaquette_GRAND_PUBLIC_vf.pdf); [DDTM 76 brochure for elected officials](https://www.seine-maritime.gouv.fr/index.php/contenu/telechargement/38431/257233/file/Plaquette_ELUS_vf.pdf):
  - The cliffs retreat about 20 cm a year on average, and more than 40 cm a year at some points.
  - Each collapse removes from a few centimetres to several metres.
  - The coast runs 140 km through 46 communes with about 300,000 inhabitants.
  - A Cerema study for the DDTM projects land losses of about 230 ha in 20 years, 300 ha in 50 years and 420 ha in 100 years.
- Two-thirds of the Normandy coastline is already concerned by rising seas, potentially affecting 428 communes, 122,000 inhabitants, 111,000 homes and 120,000 salaried jobs. The projected rise is +1.1 to +1.8 m by 2100 if warming is not held below 4 °C ‡ — [Département de la Manche, technical booklet (Oct 2024)](https://littoral.manche.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CAHIER-TECHNIQUE-RESIDENCE.pdf)
- In the Manche, 18,000 buildings are threatened by shoreline retreat, and the dune at Hauteville-sur-Mer is eroding despite riprap. The article date was not captured ‡ — [Tendance Ouest](https://www.tendanceouest.com/actualite-349215-manche-recul-du-trait-de-cote-18-000-batiments-menaces.html)
- At Agon-Coutainville, about 7 m of dune was lost in the February 2020 storms (Ciara, Dennis), after 5 m in September 2019, and 7,000 m³ of emergency re-sanding was authorised [older] ‡ — [Tendance Ouest](https://www.tendanceouest.com/actualite-353541-agon-coutainville-apres-les-tempetes-un-reensablement-de-7-000-m3.html)
- The regional strategy "Littoraux normands 2027" finds riprap, sand recharge and cliff nailing increasingly ineffective and costly, and relocating the most exposed assets increasingly urgent ‡ — [DREAL, Littoraux normands 2027 four-page summary](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/littoraux_normands_4pages-v8-2.pdf)
- The Region's "Notre Littoral pour Demain" scheme has run since 2016 and builds on the first GIEC normand results (2021), using 2050 and 2100 horizons ‡ — [Région Normandie](https://www.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/idee_notre_littoral_pour_demain.pdf)
- National benchmark: about 20% of French coasts are retreating, according to Cerema's national indicator (rates over at least 50 years, profiles every 200 m) ‡ — [Cerema](https://www.cerema.fr/fr/print/pdf/node/2061)
- Port 2000 at Le Havre, built from 2001 next to the national nature reserve and the EU bird protection zone, mainly destroyed the main high-tide roost of shorebirds. Compensation cost about €50 M (about 5% of the investment), including a €10 M artificial bird island (Ilot Reposoir, Banc du Ratier) [older] ‡ — [EEA OURCOAST case study](https://discomap.eea.europa.eu/map/Data/Milieu/OURCOAST_068_FR/OURCOAST_068_FR_Case_LeHavreHarbourCompensatoryMeasures.pdf); [INRAE, Ingénieries 2009](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-02592496v1)
- The HAROPA "chatière" project (new access channel to Port 2000) affects 74 ha of the estuary, 22 ha of which of seabed would be irreversibly artificialised (national nature protection council opinion, 2022) ‡ — [CNPN opinion](https://www.avis-biodiversite.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/2022-06-13c-00717_haropa_port_le_havre_chatiere_acces_port_2000_76_avis_du_07_2022.pdf)
- Estuary habitat loss is covered in §2: water surface from 340 km² (1750) to 142 km² (2005), a little over 1,000 ha of mudflats left, transitional waters 14% in good status.

### Inferences
- For a biodiversity datathon, the coast piste (M) is strongest when framed on estuary habitats under port and industrial pressure, which is human-driven. Cliff retreat is largely natural and climate-driven, so it suits a risk framing better than a biodiversity one.

### Gaps

## 7. Biodiversity status: regional red lists, observatory indicators, notable declines, protection

### Takeaway
Regional red lists show heavy threat levels: 39% of Normandy's breeding birds are threatened (2024 list), and 8 species are already extinct in the region. Long-term monitoring shows common farmland birds falling 18–36% since 1996 while other groups are broadly stable. Strong protection covers only about 0.5% of the region.

### Cited Findings
- Breeding birds red list (GONm, more than 512,000 records; CSRPN opinion of 9 Dec 2024; published Jan 2025): of 205 taxa, 176 were evaluated. Eight are regionally extinct, 24 critically endangered, 20 endangered and 25 vulnerable, so 39% are threatened (69 of 176), plus 7% near threatened (12 species) ‡ — [CSRPN Normandie opinion](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/avis_csrpn_2024-12-09_liste_rouge_oiseaux.pdf)
- Insect red lists from the CSRPN Normandie opinions of Jan 2022 ‡ — [CSRPN opinion 1](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/avis_2022-01-05.pdf); [CSRPN opinion 2](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/avis_2022-01-06.pdf):
  - Butterflies and burnet moths (2022): of 107 taxa, 8 regionally extinct, 3 critically endangered, 7 endangered and 9 vulnerable, so 17.7% threatened.
  - Grasshoppers and crickets, mantises and stick insects (2022): 18.8% threatened plus 10.9% near threatened.
- Reptiles (first official Normandy list, 2021; more than 59,000 records): of 11 species, only 3 are Least Concern and 4 are Near Threatened ‡ — [Liste rouge des Reptiles de Normandie](https://lashf.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Liste-rouge-des-Reptiles-de-Normandie.pdf)
- Amphibians:
  - The amphibian and reptile red lists published at the end of 2021 showed that several species had worsened. The regional action plan targets 5 species, including the yellow-bellied toad (critically endangered) and the natterjack toad and parsley frog (both vulnerable) ‡ — [CSRPN opinion on the amphibian–reptile action plan, 11 Dec 2024](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/avis_csrpn_2024-12-11_pra-amph-rept.pdf)
  - Of 15 amphibian taxa monitored in Normandy, 9 are declining. The PDF returned 404 when fetched ‡ — [Astruc et al. 2021, Bull. Soc. Herp. Fr. 178](https://lashf.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/N178_Astruc.pdf)
- Freshwater fish (former Haute-Normandie region): 31% of native species assessed are threatened, and the Seine has the most threatened species [older] ‡ — [DREAL, freshwater fish red list](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/poissons_d_eau_douce.pdf)
- Common birds, GONm "Tendances" survey (1996–2023; 87 observers, 170 routes, 403,526 records; general assembly of 6 Apr 2024) — [GONm, Bilan de 27 années de comptage](https://www.gonm.org/public/Telechargements/Etudes/tendances/2024_Tendances-bilan.pdf):
  - Populations are generally stable, except farmland birds.
  - The mean index change of common farmland species is −18% in April–May, −36.2% in June–July and −17.8% in December–January. Farmland is the only habitat group whose decline exceeds the −21% stability threshold.
  - Species examples: European greenfinch −42.5%, dunnock −53% (winter), nuthatch −41%; green woodpecker +77% and jackdaw +78% are rising.
- National benchmark: common farmland birds have fallen 33% (French STOC monitoring, to 2018) ‡ — [Reporterre](https://reporterre.net/Les-populations-d-oiseaux-des-champs-s-effondrent); [Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/stoc-suivi-temporel-des-oiseaux-communs/4-les-indicateurs/)
- Protected areas:
  - Protected areas cover 20.64% of Normandy's land surface (1 Jan 2020), but strong protection covers only 0.54% ‡ — [DREAL, Normandy protected-areas action plan 2022–2024](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/plan_d_action_sap_normandie_2022_-2024.pdf)
  - Strong protection covers less than 1% of the region, against a national target of 10% by 2030. The region has 4 regional natural parks, 15 nature reserves, 8 biological reserves and 97 Natura 2000 sites.
  - The CSRPN counts 20.9% protected and judges 22.5% by 2030 achievable. Reaching 1% strong protection by 2030 would need about 14,000 extra ha (about 2,000 ha a year), which it calls "unrealistic though desirable" ‡ — [CSRPN opinion (2022)](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/2022-01-08.pdf)

### Inferences
- The farmland-bird declines (GONm) mirror the grassland and hedge losses (§1). The bocage pistes can therefore cite a measured regional biodiversity response, not only habitat loss.
- Strong protection at 0.54%, against a 10% national target for 2030, is a crisp "blind-spot" headline for piste H. Combined with 39% of breeding birds threatened, it supports "most biodiversity lives outside strongly protected land".

### Gaps
- No percentages were retrieved from the flora red lists (Basse-Normandie 2015 by the CBN de Brest, and Haute-Normandie), and no unified Normandy flora figure.
- The mammal red list figures (CSRPN opinion of 13 Jun 2022) were not retrieved, and no wild-bee or pollinator red list for Normandy was found.

## 8. Equine sector: horses, equine holdings, associated grassland, national rank

### Takeaway
Normandy is France's leading horse region, with about 146,000–147,000 equines. It ranks first for births and is home to 1 in 6 French breeders. Equines use about 92,600 ha, or 5% of farmland. This grassland-dependent activity is a potential ally of bocage and grassland conservation rather than a pressure.

### Cited Findings

Normandy is the leading region for annual births and the leading economic region for the sector (17,400 jobs, €850 M) ‡ — [Observatoire filière équine 2025](https://chevaux-normandie.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Observatoire-filiere-equine-2025.pdf)

## 10. Mapping of candidate pistes A–N to the most acute and best-documented Normandy issues

### Takeaway
The most acute and best-documented Normandy issues are:
1. Loss of hedgerows and permanent grassland.
2. Water pollution from farming.
3. Continuing, inefficient land take.
4. Collapsing farmland and aquatic biodiversity, with very little strong protection.

Pistes C, B, E, I and H target these directly (high). Pistes F, G, M, A, J, L and D address real but narrower or less-quantified issues (medium). Pistes K and N are the least tied to an acute pressure (low).

### Cited Findings
Pitch-ready headline figures, one or two per issue (full sourcing in §§1–9):
- Hedges: about 158,000 km today, −50% since 1972, and a density twice the national one (53 against 27 m/ha) — [DREAL/ANBDD 2024](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/lineaireshaies-normandie-2024_anbdd.pdf) ‡; [Canal-U 2025](https://www.canal-u.tv/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2025/05/PRESENTATION%20COLLOQUE.pdf) ‡
- Manche: hedge density halved, 175 to 87 m/ha (1972–2015) — [GIEC normand 2025](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/giec2_haies_bocage_vf_22janv2025.pdf) ‡
- Grasslands: −103,000 ha (about −11%) in 2008–2019, and the steepest drop in the grassland ratio of any region (−6.45%, 2018 reference to 2023) — [Agreste 2021](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/Agreste_Etudes_no6-Prairies_Normandie_2008_2019-septembre_2021_cle09a9b1.pdf); [Réussir 2023](https://www.reussir.fr/lait/prairies-permanentes-4-regions-en-regime-dautorisation-ou-dinterdiction)
- Groundwater: chemical status not good at 60% of points, against 31% nationally — [INSEE 2020](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/4629011)
- Rivers: 18–23% in good status (provisional 2025, Seine-Normandie basin) — [CESER 2025](https://www.ceser.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2025-03/Consultation%20eau%20CESER%20Web_2.pdf) ‡
- Drinking water: 98 priority catchments, 43% of them in poor state for nitrates or pesticides — [DREAL 2020](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/20200623_captage-ep_vf.pdf) ‡
- Land take: 17,700 ha in 2011–2021 (71% housing), with median EPCI consumption of 193 ha against 134 ha in France — [France Stratégie 2024](https://www.strategie-plan.gouv.fr/files/2024-10/fs-na_zan-focus_region_normandie_final.pdf)
- Artificialisation: +29,287 ha in 2008–2023, re-accelerating, and 2023 the second-worst year — [Agreste 2026](https://draaf.normandie.agriculture.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/acces-etudes-19_isag-oscom.pdf)
- Birds: 39% of breeding birds threatened (2024 red list) — [CSRPN 2024](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/avis_csrpn_2024-12-09_liste_rouge_oiseaux.pdf) ‡; common farmland birds −18% to −36% since 1996 — [GONm 2024](https://www.gonm.org/public/Telechargements/Etudes/tendances/2024_Tendances-bilan.pdf)
- Protection: strong protection on 0.54% of the land (2020), against a 10% national target for 2030 — [DREAL](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/plan_d_action_sap_normandie_2022_-2024.pdf) ‡
- Salmon: −72% (2023–24 against 2010–19), and fishing banned basin-wide — [DRIEAT](https://www.drieat.ile-de-france.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/note_presentation_consultation_public_26-27.pdf) ‡
- Sélune: young salmon born upstream in 2023, after the removal of the 36 m and 16 m dams — [Sciences Ouest 2023](https://espace-sciences.org/sciences-ouest/414/actualite/le-retour-des-poissons-dans-la-selune)
- Coast: chalk cliffs retreat about 20 cm a year — [DDTM 76](https://www.seine-maritime.gouv.fr/index.php/contenu/telechargement/38432/257237/file/Plaquette_GRAND_PUBLIC_vf.pdf) ‡; two-thirds of the coast is exposed to sea-level rise, concerning 122,000 inhabitants — [Manche 2024](https://littoral.manche.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/CAHIER-TECHNIQUE-RESIDENCE.pdf) ‡
- Seine estuary: water surface reduced from 340 to 142 km² (1750–2005) — [GIP Seine-Aval](https://www.normandie.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/gipsa_indicateurs_dta_2017.pdf) ‡
- Brownfields: about 5,100 sites covering about 3,300 ha — [EPF Normandie 2026](https://cdn.netboard.me/151942/files/684d8ffb563dd10dd0fe726ccacad56d.pdf) ‡
- Horses: about 147,000 equines, the leading region — [IFCE 2023](https://www.ifce.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/cpresse-journees-decouvertes-Normandie2023.pdf); 92,600 ha of farmland used by equines (5%) — [Observatoire 2025](https://chevaux-normandie.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Observatoire-filiere-equine-2025.pdf) ‡

- The strongest combinations are C + B (+ L) for bocage and grassland restoration priorities, E + B for water-driven restoration, I + A (+ L) for land take, brownfields and ZAN scenarios, and H + B for protection gaps.
- Normandy-versus-France contrasts carry the most weight in a 5-minute pitch:
  - hedge density 53 against 27 m/ha
  - groundwater not good at 60% against 31% of points
  - median EPCI land take 193 against 134 ha
  - artificialised land 11% against 9%
  - organic farming about 7% against 10.7%
  - the steepest grassland-ratio drop of any region (−6.45%)
