## 1. EU Nature Restoration Regulation (2024/1991) and France's restoration plan

### In short
The NRR (in force since 18 Aug 2024) needs member states to map and quantify where restoration will happen. Each national restoration plan (NRP) must include "indicative maps of potential areas to be restored". France sent its draft NRP, "Agir pour restaurer la nature", on 9 Oct 2026, over a month after the 1 Sept 2026 deadline. The draft leans on existing policies and on State–Region coordination, with monitoring mainly through the regional biodiversity committees (CRB). The Commission's observations are expected in spring 2027 and the final plan by about Sept 2027. 2026–2028 is therefore exactly the window in which regions will need spatial prioritisation and monitoring tools.

### Sources
#### Regulation and targets
- The regulation entered into force on 18 Aug 2024. The Commission proposal dates from 22 June 2022, Parliament agreed the text on 27 Feb 2024 and the Council adopted it on 17 June 2024 — [European Commission NRR page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/nature-and-biodiversity/nature-restoration-regulation_en)
- Overarching EU target: restoration measures covering at least 20% of EU land and 20% of sea areas by 2030, and all ecosystems in need of restoration by 2050. Obligations come with 2030/2040 milestones for terrestrial, coastal and freshwater habitats, marine, urban, rivers and floodplains, pollinators, agricultural and forest ecosystems — [Irish Climate Change Advisory Council NRR factsheet (2026)](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf)
- Habitats (Art. 4, Annex I): member states must bring habitat areas not in good condition back to good condition, with 2030/2040/2050 targets; re-establish habitats where they no longer occur; restore habitats of Birds/Habitats Directive species; and prevent significant deterioration — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf). Press summary of the habitat target: "one third restored by 2030", all by 2050 — [Reporterre, 15 Oct 2026](https://reporterre.net/Le-plan-pour-la-nature-de-la-France-n-a-aucune-ambition-malgre-l-insistance-de-l-Europe). The exact 30%/60%/90% percentages (2030/2040/2050) and the Natura 2000 priority are in the legal text — [EUR-Lex 2024/1991](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1991/oj/eng) (not verified verbatim; see Open points).
- Urban (Art. 8): no net loss of total national urban green space or urban tree canopy cover in urban ecosystem areas by 2030; then an increasing trend in urban green space after 2030 and in tree canopy in each urban ecosystem area until satisfactory levels — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf)
- Rivers (Art. 9): inventory artificial barriers; identify barriers to remove so as to contribute to the EU objective of at least 25,000 km of free-flowing rivers by 2030; remove barriers per the NRP, giving priority to obsolete barriers; improve floodplain natural functions by 2050 — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf); [Commission page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/nature-and-biodiversity/nature-restoration-regulation_en)
- Pollinators (Art. 10): improve pollinator diversity and reverse the decline by 2030, then an increasing trend — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf)
- Agriculture (Art. 11):
  - an increasing national trend in at least 2 of 3 indicators: grassland butterfly index, organic carbon stock in cropland mineral soils, share of agricultural land with high-diversity landscape features;
  - the common farmland bird index must reach specified levels;
  - drained peatlands in agricultural use: at least 30% restored by 2030 (at least a quarter rewetted), 40% by 2040 and 50% by 2050 (at least a third rewetted).
  - [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf). Press: farmland birds +10% by 2030 for France — [Reporterre](https://reporterre.net/Le-plan-pour-la-nature-de-la-France-n-a-aucune-ambition-malgre-l-insistance-de-l-Europe)
- Forests (Art. 12): increasing trend in the common forest bird index and in 6 of 7 forest indicators. Art. 13: contribute to 3 billion additional trees EU-wide by 2030 — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf)
- Annex VII lists example restoration measures that map directly onto the pistes — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf):
  - #1 rewet drained peatlands; #4 paludiculture;
  - #6 remove longitudinal and lateral barriers; #9 riparian buffers;
  - #16 high-diversity landscape features "such as … hedgerows" in arable land and intensive grassland;
  - #18 re-establish extensive grazing; #20 "stop ploughing grassland";
  - #31 increase urban green spaces, including hedges;
  - #33 "Convert brownfield sites, former industrial areas and quarries into natural sites".
- Flexibilities — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf):
  - Art. 27 allows temporary suspension of the agricultural provisions after exceptional EU-wide events affecting food-production land;
  - Arts 6–7 presume renewable energy and defence projects to be of overriding public interest;
  - Art. 11(4) lets peat-extraction sites and other land uses count towards the peatland targets.

#### National restoration plans: content and EU calendar
- NRP content (Art. 15) includes:
  - quantification of the areas to be restored, with "indicative maps of potential areas to be restored";
  - the restoration measures, specifying which fall within Natura 2000;
  - the agricultural and forest indicators chosen;
  - monitoring and effectiveness assessment;
  - climate and land-degradation co-benefits and socio-economic impacts;
  - financing needs and means, and subsidies that harm the targets;
  - a summary of the participation process.
  - [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf)
- Calendar — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf):
  - draft NRP due 1 Sept 2026; the Commission assesses it within 6 months;
  - the final plan follows within 6 months of the Commission's observations;
  - plans cover the period to 2050 and are revised in 2032 and 2042;
  - member states report progress by 30 June 2028 and at least every 3 years thereafter; the EEA produces an EU-wide overview every 6 years.
- The factsheet notes that the regulation stresses GIS, remote sensing, AI and citizen science as the basis for monitoring, and that high-quality baseline data is critical — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf) (Irish authors' reading of the regulation).
- Commission statement of 1 Sept 2026: member states submitted draft NRPs. The Commission and the EEA will assess them and send observations, which will be made public, and final plans are due within a year. Many member states are building their plans with regional and local input, and many regions and municipalities already run restoration programmes. For the 2028–2034 MFF, the Commission has proposed explicitly counting NRPs towards the 35% climate-and-environment spending benchmark — [European Commission news, 1 Sept 2026](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-countries-submit-draft-plans-restoring-nature-2026-09-01_en)
- Conflicting account: on 22 Sept 2026 FACE reported that only a few member states met the deadline, listing Austria, Bulgaria, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal, Spain and Sweden as having submitted. MEPs at the 1 Sept ENVI hearing stressed the lack of restoration funding and the delayed Commission report on financing needs — [FACE, 22 Sept 2026](https://www.face.eu/2026/09/nature-restoration-plans-late-and-underfunded/). The Commission report on EU financing (Art. 21(7), due by Aug 2025) was "not publicly available" at the time the Irish factsheet was written — [factsheet](https://www.climatecouncil.ie/councilpublications/secretariatfactsheets/Final%20EU%20Nature%20Restoration%20Regulation%20Factsheet%20web.pdf)

#### France's draft plan "Agir pour restaurer la nature"
- Preparation: a 9 May 2025 press release announced the plan and a CNDP-supervised preliminary consultation (concertation préalable, 23 May–23 Aug 2025), with regional citizen events called "La nature près de chez vous" — [ecologie.gouv.fr press release](https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/presse/france-prepare-son-plan-national-agir-restaurer-nature-invite-francais-participer-son); [ecologie.gouv.fr consultation page](https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/rendez-vous/agir-restaurer-nature-participez-concertation)
- An arrêté dated 27 Aug 2026 (JO of 3 Sept 2026) subjects the plan "Agir pour restaurer la nature" to environmental assessment — [AIDA/INERIS legal database (excerpt only)](https://aida.ineris.fr/node/46395)
- France submitted its draft on 9 Oct 2026, over a month after the 1 Sept deadline — [Reporterre, 15 Oct 2026](https://reporterre.net/Le-plan-pour-la-nature-de-la-France-n-a-aucune-ambition-malgre-l-insistance-de-l-Europe); [Ecolobizz, 17 Oct 2026](https://ecolobizz.fr/2026/10/17/restauration-de-la-nature-paris-a-presente-a-bruxelles-son-plan-national-avec-un-peu-de-retard/)
- Content as reported in the press — [Ecolobizz](https://ecolobizz.fr/2026/10/17/restauration-de-la-nature-paris-a-presente-a-bruxelles-son-plan-national-avec-un-peu-de-retard/); [Reporterre](https://reporterre.net/Le-plan-pour-la-nature-de-la-France-n-a-aucune-ambition-malgre-l-insistance-de-l-Europe):
  - Agriculture: 4 Mha under restoration measures by 2030 (about 13.8% of French farmland, per Reporterre), including fewer pesticides and mineral fertilisers, more extensive livestock farming and hedge planting. The government ties this to the Loi d'orientation agricole (LOA) targets of 21% organic area and +50,000 km of hedges net by 2030.
  - Forests: 5 Mha by 2030.
  - Rivers and wetlands: recreate at least 15,913 ha of rivers and 19,526 ha of wetlands; remove more than 1,700 obstacles to river flow. Targets for free-flowing rivers and drained peatlands are deferred to the 2031 revision.
  - Marine: about 66,700 ha of marine habitats restored and 9,500 ha recreated.
  - Specific objectives for dunes and heathland.
  - Urban: preserve green space and tree cover, with no net decline in the municipalities concerned.
  - Pollinators and birds: handled mainly through Écophyto 2030.
- Governance as reported: implementation relies on the Natura 2000 network, local authorities (collectivités) and State–Region coordination; monitoring falls mainly to the regional biodiversity committees (CRB) and territorial bodies — [Ecolobizz](https://ecolobizz.fr/2026/10/17/restauration-de-la-nature-paris-a-presente-a-bruxelles-son-plan-national-avec-un-peu-de-retard/)
- Cost and financing (sources conflict):
  - Ecolobizz: about €2bn per year — [Ecolobizz](https://ecolobizz.fr/2026/10/17/restauration-de-la-nature-paris-a-presente-a-bruxelles-son-plan-national-avec-un-peu-de-retard/)
  - Reporterre: needs of about €5bn, about €3.1bn already mobilised by the State, the rest expected from EU, private and philanthropic funds, plus a national committee of biodiversity funders to be created by end 2026 — [Reporterre](https://reporterre.net/Le-plan-pour-la-nature-de-la-France-n-a-aucune-ambition-malgre-l-insistance-de-l-Europe)
- Criticism: five NGOs (FNE, SNPN, LPO, Surfrider Europe, Humanité & Biodiversité) say the plan bundles existing policies with no new measures before 2030. The national biodiversity committee (CNB) gave a critical opinion on an earlier draft on 25 June. Next steps: Commission observations expected spring 2027; finalisation by Sept 2027, after the Autorité environnementale opinion and public participation — [Reporterre](https://reporterre.net/Le-plan-pour-la-nature-de-la-France-n-a-aucune-ambition-malgre-l-insistance-de-l-Europe); [Ecolobizz](https://ecolobizz.fr/2026/10/17/restauration-de-la-nature-paris-a-presente-a-bruxelles-son-plan-national-avec-un-peu-de-retard/)

### Our reading
- The NRP requirement for "indicative maps of potential areas to be restored" and the 2028 reporting obligation create concrete demand for regional prioritisation maps (pistes B, G, F, A) and indicator pipelines (D). France's plan delegates monitoring to the CRBs and relies on State–Region coordination, so in Normandy the natural "client" body for an NRR-oriented tool is the CRB (co-chaired by the Région president and the préfet, see §3), served by DREAL, the Région and ANBDD.
- France's draft defers free-flowing river and drained-peatland targets to the 2031 revision. In the short term, peatland/wetland (G) and river (F) tools would support the existing programmes that feed the NRP (the regional peatland plan, AESN aids) rather than a national quantified target. This is a nuance worth stating honestly in the pitch.
- The farmland-bird "+10% by 2030" figure suggests France sits in the regulation's group of member states with "historically more depleted" farmland bird populations (Annex V; 110% of baseline by 2030). This is an inference: Annex V was not verified verbatim.
- The NRR's "high-diversity landscape features" indicator (Art. 11) and Annex VII measure #16 (hedgerows) give hedge/bocage tools (C, L, N) a direct EU anchor. Annex VII #33 does the same for brownfield renaturation (A).

### Open points
- Article-level percentages (Art. 4: 30/60/90%; Art. 8 baseline year 2024; Annex V country list) were not verified verbatim: EUR-Lex returned no usable text and the factsheet does not give them.
- The official text of France's draft NRP was not found online. Content comes from press accounts (Ecolobizz, Reporterre), which disagree on costs (€2bn per year versus about €5bn in needs).
- No source was found on any adopted EU amendment or delay to the NRR ("omnibus"). The Commission's 1 Sept 2026 communication shows implementation proceeding. The count of member states that met the deadline is disputed (Commission versus FACE).
- No document was found specifying how France will regionalise the NRP (for example, regional restoration roadmaps). Only the press mention of the CRBs' monitoring role and State–Region coordination.

## 2. French national frameworks

### In short
National ambition is intact on paper: SNB 2030, +50,000 km of hedges net by 2030, 10% strong protection, and ZAN with a halving of land take over 2021–2031. Delivery money is being cut, though. The national hedge budget fell from €110M announced for 2024 to €7M proposed for 2026 and €1.3M proposed for 2027. The Fonds vert fell from €2.5bn (not checked against a source) to €1.15bn in 2025 and about €850M in 2026, with €1bn proposed for 2027. ZAN reform remains unresolved: the Senate's TRACE bill is still pending at the Assemblée, and the Conseil constitutionnel struck down new ZAN exemptions on 21 May 2026. Two operational novelties matter for the pistes. First, from 1 Oct 2026 every hedge destruction requires a single prior declaration (régime unique de la haie, decree of 28 Aug 2026). Second, regional COPs are now the main frame for territorial ecological planning.

#### Hedges: the new legal regime (operational from 1 Oct 2026)
- Legal basis: Loi n° 2025-268 du 24 mars 2025 (LOSARGA/LOA), arts 37–38, created arts L.412-21 ff. of the code de l'environnement. Implementing decree: Décret n° 2026-829 du 28 août 2026 relatif au régime unique de la haie. It applies to declarations filed from 1 Oct 2026 in mainland France — [Landot avocats blog, 2 Sept 2026](https://blog.landot-avocats.net/2026/09/02/le-regime-unique-de-la-haie-apres-la-loi-de-mars-2025-et-le-decret-daout-2026/)
- Legal definition: a linear unit of vegetation at most 20 m wide with at least two of shrubs, trees or other woody plants.
- Procedure: a single prior declaration to the préfet through the online "guichet unique de la haie" (haie.beta.gouv.fr), which at the time of writing covered departments 02 and 14 (Calvados) and was expected to become national. The declaration replaces about ten regimes (Natura 2000, protected species, CAP conditionality, local planning protections, etc.). Silence for two months means non-opposition.
- Compensation and controls: any destruction requires replanting at least an equal length, with a coefficient set by prefectoral order. A nesting-season works ban of at least 21 weeks applies. Préfets must publish online maps of hedge protections.
- [Landot avocats blog](https://blog.landot-avocats.net/2026/09/02/le-regime-unique-de-la-haie-apres-la-loi-de-mars-2025-et-le-decret-daout-2026/) (law-firm summary; not verified against Légifrance). Draft decree and CNPN opinion (19 Nov 2025) — [consultation file](https://www.consultations-publiques.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/tecl2521973d_projet_de_cret_haies.pdf)

### Our reading
- With national hedge money near zero in 2027, regional (FEDER) and water-agency funding become the main levers for hedges. This strengthens the case for tools that help the Région target its own "Opération Normandie Haies" (pistes C, L, N).
- The new hedge regime creates an administrative flow: declarations, compensation obligations and prefectoral protection maps. A monitoring tool could cross-check it against observed hedge loss and verify compensation plantings (C).
- ZAN is legally unchanged in substance as of Oct 2026 (the 2021 and 2023 laws apply; the CC censured the 2026 loosening) but politically contested. A land-take tool (I) should be framed as "quality of land sobriety" and "where not to build or where to renature", not as policing.
- Not checked against a source: renaturation counts as désartificialisation in the net ZAN balance (code de l'urbanisme L.101-2-1); SCoT can designate zones préférentielles de renaturation; and the loi Industrie verte (2023) created sites naturels de compensation, de restauration et de renaturation. All three would strengthen pistes A and I if confirmed.

## 3. Normandy frameworks and actors

### In short
Normandy has an unusually explicit set of hooks. The COP Normandie 2026 roadmap (57 actions, co-signed by the préfet and Région president Hervé Morin) contains named ecosystem actions with named pilots:
- EPCI-level bocage action plans (Région);
- a regional peatland action plan (PNR Marais du Cotentin et du Bessin + CEN);
- ecological trajectories for each habitat type to prefigure restoration scenarios (Conservatoire botanique);
- centralising renaturation projects (ANBDD);
- integrating rehabilitated brownfields into EPFN's online observatory (EPFN).

The Région itself runs FEDER-funded calls for hedges ("Opération Normandie Haies", with a zero-net-loss-of-hedges-by-2030 target) and for green and blue network (TVB) restoration works. It also renewed its brownfield partnership with EPFN for 2026–2030 (€85M). The SRADDET's ZAN rule gives each territory a reduction rate between −42% and −62%. These are the strongest anchors for the datathon pitch.

### Our reading
- Named COP pilots are the most credible "clients" for scale-up, because they hold an official mandate and a roadmap milestone:
  - Région for bocage/EPCI hedge plans;
  - PNR MCB and CEN for peatlands;
  - CBN for habitat restoration trajectories;
  - ANBDD for renaturation projects and carbon knowledge;
  - EPFN for the brownfield observatory;
  - DREAL/AESN for water;
  - Chambre d'agriculture for agricultural practice, "remembrement environnemental" and biomass.
  A pistes pitch that names the COP action it accelerates and its pilot will read as "ready to adopt" for regional officials.
- The Région has three directly controllable levers: FEDER OS 2.7 calls (hedges, TVB), the EPFN convention, and the SRADDET/ZAN conference. Pistes that improve targeting of these instruments (A, B, C, I, L) give the Région an immediate use.
- AESN funds "environmental monitoring and data collection" up to 80%. This is a plausible post-datathon funding route for water-related tools (E, F, G, M), timed after the Jan 2027 platform restart.
- The Région's hedge target (zero net loss by 2030) is less ambitious than the national one (+50,000 km net gain nationally). A tool showing the net trajectory per EPCI against both targets is policy-relevant and neutral.

- The AESN 12th programme envelope is disputed (€4.7bn on the AESN site versus €3.14bn in a CEN presentation). Specific AESN Normandy territorial priorities (Seine-Aval & rivières normandes, Bocages normands) were not detailed.

## 4. Piste by piste (A–N): policy hooks, clients, scale-up and relevance

### In short
Highest relevance for a jury of regional elected officials:
- A, Friches → Nature;
- B, multi-criteria restoration map;
- C, bocage;
- G, restorable wetlands and peatlands;
- I, "what concrete ate" (relevant but politically sensitive).

Each plugs into a named COP Normandie action with an identified pilot and into a Région-controlled funding instrument (EPFN convention, FEDER hedge or TVB calls, SRADDET). Each also has an explicit NRR anchor: Annex VII #33, Art. 15 maps, Art. 11 landscape features and peatlands, Art. 8.

Medium relevance: D, E, F, H, J, L, M, N. These are policy-relevant but the Région is not the lead competence (E, F, M), the tool risks duplicating existing products (D, F), or it is politically delicate or data-poor (J, N). L and D become stronger as layers on top of C or A.

Low as a standalone: K. It suits citizen engagement better than decision support for a regional jury.

### Our reading (clients, scale-up needs and relevance per piste)

#### A. Friches → Nature
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - EPF Normandie: owner of the observatory and its scoring tool; the natural maintainer, adding an "ecological renaturation potential" score;
  - Région: co-funder of the 2026–2030 convention;
  - ANBDD: COP pilot for centralising renaturation projects;
  - EPCI/SCoT: planning documents, ZAN balance;
  - DDT(M) and Préfecture: Fonds vert;
  - AESN: de-sealing aid;
  - CEN: post-renaturation management;
  - Conservatoire du littoral, for coastal sites.
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - a data and integration agreement with EPFN, whose detailed observatory is restricted to local authorities and the State (public access only via Cartofriches);
  - differentiation from EPFN's existing multi-use scoring: add ecological criteria such as TVB connectivity, flood and runoff role, soil, urban heat, proximity to reservoirs and species;
  - site validation (pollution, land ownership);
  - a cost model per hectare renatured, set against funding (EPFN/Région 60–80% coverage, AESN €25–100 per m², Fonds vert);
  - an annual refresh synced with the observatory.
- **Relevance: HIGH.** It directly serves a named COP action (EPFN), a Région-funded convention (€85M for 2026–2030), ZAN and NRR Annex VII #33. Elected officials also see the territorial-development payoff of "eyesore to green asset". With only 48 of 1,332 reconversion projects aimed at renaturation, there is visible room to act.

#### B. Multi-criteria restoration priority map (stakes × degradation × opportunity)
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - DREAL Normandie, for regional input to the NRP;
  - the CRB (co-chaired by the Région president and the préfet), as validation forum;
  - CBN Normandie, COP pilot for habitat trajectories;
  - Région: SRB, and targeting of the FEDER TVB works call;
  - ANBDD: observatory and hosting;
  - CEN, PNRs and OFB, as end users.
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - co-building the weighting with CBN, CSRPN and DREAL so it fits the NRP's "indicative maps" logic;
  - access to habitat-condition data (CBN) and sensitive species data (regional SINP platform, under agreement);
  - transparent, adjustable criteria, because elected officials must be able to see trade-offs;
  - an update cycle aligned with NRP finalisation (about Sept 2027) and reporting (30 June 2028, then every 3 years);
  - integration in ANBDD's observatory.
- **Relevance: HIGH.** It is the most direct answer to P1 and is timed exactly with the NRP. The main risk is overlap with CBN or DREAL work in progress, so pitch it as the prototype that accelerates the COP Axe 4 action, not a competitor to it.

#### C. Normandy bocage over 70 years
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - Région: the "Opération Normandie Haies" team and the COP Axe 2 pilot;
  - EPCI: 3-year action plans;
  - PNRs (Perche, Normandie-Maine, MCB, BSN);
  - Chambres d'agriculture: PGDH advisers, "remembrement environnemental";
  - DDT(M): new hedge regime, targeting controls and compensation;
  - DRAAF: Pacte haie;
  - AESN: erosion and runoff;
  - ANBDD: observatory.
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - an agreed hedge reference layer and change-detection method, validated by field partners;
  - linking to the flows of the new hedge regime (declarations via the guichet unique, protection maps, compensation plantings) to monitor net balance;
  - an EPCI-level dashboard against the 2030 zero-net-loss target, plugged into Normandie Haies reporting;
  - funding through FEDER OS 2.7 (to 2027/2030) or AESN soft-hydraulics budget lines;
  - collection of the "Normandie haies" pilot results (6 territories).
- **Relevance: HIGH.** The bocage is iconic to Normandy. The tool serves a Région-run programme with a 2030 target and EPCI action plans, and links nature to agriculture, erosion and the bocage-wood economy. National funding collapse (€1.3M proposed for 2027) makes regional targeting more valuable.

#### D. Biodiversity bulletin per EPCI (on the 68 COP indicators)
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - ANBDD: Observatoire de la Biodiversité Normandie, as maintainer;
  - Région and the State COP secretariat (Préfecture/SGAR): COP monitoring;
  - EPCI: SCoT, PLUi and TEN applications;
  - CRB.
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - an automated pipeline from the Région's 68-indicator dataset;
  - statistical robustness at EPCI scale, since observation effort biases species indicators;
  - editorial co-design with ANBDD so it reads as advisory, not ranking;
  - a hosting and maintenance agreement;
  - a clear link to COP milestones and the 2028 NRR reporting.
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** It reuses the Région's own COP indicators, answers P2 and fits the annual COP review. It risks looking like "another dashboard" that overlaps ANBDD's observatory unless it adds interpretation (EPCI typologies, recommended actions, links to available aid).

#### E. Water × agriculture pressure–impact by river basin
- **Clients / maintainers**: AESN (its territorial directions); DREAL; DDT(M); SAGE/CLE and river syndicates (GEMAPI); Chambres d'agriculture; Bio en Normandie and DRAAF (PSE).
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - alignment with AESN/SDAGE pressure–impact methods (risk of duplicating the SDAGE état des lieux);
  - acceptability to farmers (frame around PSE and support, not blame);
  - AESN "connaissance" funding (monitoring and data up to 80%) after the platform restart in Jan 2027;
  - links to the 13 volumes-prélevables studies.
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** The stakes are strong (42% of water bodies in good status; COP water actions), but the Région is not the lead competence, and AESN or the State already produce pressure–impact diagnostics.

#### F. River continuity: prioritise obstacle removal
- **Clients / maintainers**: AESN; DREAL and OFB; DDT(M) (police de l'eau); GEMAPI syndicates; fishing federations; PNRs.
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - building on the existing PAPARCE priority list to 2027 rather than re-prioritising from scratch;
  - adding multi-benefit criteria (flood, sediment, tourism, heritage) to ease acceptability, mills being a contested issue;
  - site-level hydraulic studies;
  - maintenance by OFB or AESN.
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** There is a clear EU hook (NRR Art. 9) and a Normandy flagship (Sélune), but the competence sits with the State and AESN, the priorities already exist, France deferred quantified free-flowing targets to 2031, and the subject is politically sensitive for local officials.

#### G. Restorable wetlands (potential wetlands under crops or artificial land)
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - PNR Marais du Cotentin et du Bessin and CEN: COP peatland plan pilots;
  - AESN: wetlands aid up to 80%, including acquisition and ORE;
  - DREAL;
  - Conservatoire du littoral and GIP Seine-Aval, for the estuary's wet grasslands;
  - GEMAPI EPCI and SAGE;
  - Chambres d'agriculture and SAFER, for voluntary measures and land;
  - Région: carbon co-benefits in the COP.
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - field validation (soils, hydrology);
  - a voluntary landowner engagement model (PSE, paludiculture, acquisition);
  - integration into the PRAT 2026–2035 actions;
  - carbon accounting, consistent with the COP carbon-knowledge action;
  - prioritising sites where NRR peatland targets and AESN aid overlap.
- **Relevance: HIGH.** It plugs straight into a named COP action and pilot, into the NRR peatland target and into generous AESN aid. Carbon and flood co-benefits appeal to elected officials. Avoid framing it as taking farmland.

#### H. Blind spots of protection (hotspots outside protected areas)
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - DREAL: owner of the regional protected-areas plan (PAT), due for update;
  - Région: SRB, and creation of regional nature reserves (RNR), not checked against a source;
  - Départements: ENS;
  - CEN and Conservatoire du littoral (acquisition);
  - OFB; CSRPN (validation).
- **Scale-up needs**: access to sensitive species data (SINP/CBN); expert validation; alignment with the PAT update and its target of about +14,000 ha of strong protection by 2030; a careful communication strategy towards landowners.
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** The gap is concrete (about 0.5% strong protection against a 1% target) and fits the SRB, but "more protection" speaks less to territorial development than restoration or valorisation, and the tool mainly serves State services.

#### I. "What concrete ate" (artificialisation of hedges, grasslands, ZNIEFF and wetlands per EPCI versus the ZAN trajectory)
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - Région: SRADDET monitoring, the regional governance conference and the annual ENAF review;
  - DREAL and DDT(M);
  - EPFN (land observatory);
  - agences d'urbanisme (AURH and others);
  - SCoT and EPCI preparing PLUi compliance;
  - CESER.
- **Scale-up needs**:
  - consistency with official consumption baselines (INSEE/Cerema land files versus the regional EPFN base; flag the 8,400 ha versus 6,000 ha discrepancy);
  - a method robust enough for local officials' scrutiny;
  - framing as "qualitative sobriety: where not to build, where to compensate or renature";
  - a regular update cycle (annual ENAF review);
  - legal neutrality given the ZAN reform uncertainty.
- **Relevance: HIGH, but sensitive.** ZAN is the land-use issue regional and local officials care about most. The tool adds an ecological-quality layer that existing quantity-only tools lack, and it supports SCoT revisions under way (12 SCoT). It must avoid an accusatory tone while ZAN is politically contested.

#### J. Coherence of public action (needs × efforts per EPCI)
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - Préfecture/SGAR: COP secretariat, DSIL and Fonds vert allocation already "prioritised by the COP roadmap";
  - Région: FEDER managing authority, targeting calls;
  - ANBDD: TEN and ABC promotion;
  - AESN; CESER (evaluation).
- **Scale-up needs**: aggregating funding data across schemes (Fonds vert, FEDER, AESN, OFB ABC) at EPCI level; agreed "need" indicators; governance of how results are used (targeting rather than judging); periodic refresh tied to calls.
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** It speaks to elected officials' concern for equity and efficiency of public money and to P2 ("guide public policy"). Exposing under- or over-funded territories is politically delicate, and the underlying data is heterogeneous.

#### K. Citizen interface "Ma commune et sa nature"
- **Clients / maintainers**: ANBDD; communes (new mandates since the March 2026 municipal elections, not checked against a source); PNRs; associations; OFB (ABC programme); Région communication.
- **Scale-up needs**: a hosting and moderation budget; protection of sensitive species locations; content partnerships; clear differentiation from national commune-level portals.
- **Relevance: LOW as a standalone.** It has engagement value (NRR citizen science, ABC, TEN) but weak fit with "decision-support for regional elected officials". It works better as the public front end of D or C.

#### L. Scenario simulator (hedges planted, brownfields renatured → indicators)
- **Clients / maintainers**:
  - Région: COP steering and arbitration between programmes;
  - ANBDD and GIP Seine-Aval: COP carbon-knowledge pilots;
  - Métropole Rouen Normandie: carbon-in-planning experiment;
  - EPCI: PCAET and SCoT.
- **Scale-up needs**: scientifically defensible response coefficients (validated by CBN, ANBDD, university and GIEC normand networks); uncertainty display; maintenance of parameters (costs from AESN, EPFN and FEDER rates); anchoring to official targets (2030 hedge balance, ZAN envelopes, NRR milestones).
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** Officials value "what if, at what cost, with what impact", which matches the pitch's "impacts" question, but credibility depends on validated coefficients. It is strongest as a module on top of C or A.

#### M. Coast and Seine estuary pressures
- **Clients / maintainers**: DREAL (mission estuaire); GIP Seine-Aval (Seine-Aval 8, 2027–2032); HAROPA PORT (natural-heritage master plan); Conservatoire du littoral; Région (coastal adaptation, COP); coastal EPCI; AESN (coast aid).
- **Scale-up needs**: plugging into the Seine-Aval 8 co-construction and the STRASS monitoring strategy; access to marine and estuarine data; port and industry acceptability; a narrow, decision-specific focus (for example mudflat or wet-grassland restoration opportunities against port land) to avoid superficiality.
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** The economic stakes are high (Axe Seine, HAROPA) and the timing is good (Seine-Aval 8 being prepared, May 2025 scientific call for a restoration plan), but the topic is scientifically complex and already covered by GIP Seine-Aval, so a six-week MVP risks being thin.

#### N. Horses and grasslands (equine sector and bocage)
- **Clients / maintainers**: Région (equine policy, agriculture); Conseil des Chevaux de Normandie; Chambres d'agriculture; DRAAF (BCAE 1); PNRs Perche and Normandie-Maine (equine and bocage territories); ANBDD.
- **Scale-up needs**: access to equine land-use data (privacy constraints); a partnership with the equine sector; an economic narrative (grasslands as a production asset, possible labelling); validation of grassland-loss figures (CSRPN motion).
- **Relevance: MEDIUM.** It is original and Normandy-specific, and crosses an economic sector the Région funds (€6M per year) with grasslands and bocage, which is exactly the challenge's "economy × nature" ask. But it is niche, the equine policy has no explicit grassland measure, and the data is patchy. It could rise to high if combined with C ("bocage and herbage economies").
